Build a Balanced Study Plan

Begin by checking the current official AISSEE information for the target class and admission year. Use the published syllabus and exam pattern to organise topics, then divide the available preparation time into manageable weekly goals. A simple notebook can track the date, topic practised, mistakes made and the next revision date. This helps students revisit weak areas rather than repeatedly practising only familiar questions.

During a mock test, practise following the time limit and reading each question carefully. After the test, spend time understanding why an answer was incorrect: the concept may be unclear, the question may have been misread, or the calculation may need checking. Reviewing mistakes is an important part of preparation. Keep the routine realistic and leave enough time for schoolwork, sleep and recreation.

A Practical Preparation Routine

Consistent practice is more useful than trying to cover everything at the last minute. Students can follow a routine that balances concept learning, timed practice, review and rest. The precise subjects and question pattern depend on the class and the applicable All India Sainik Schools Entrance Examination (AISSEE) notification, so families should always check the current official information before planning around an exam year.

Suggested weekly cycle
  1. Revise core concepts and note doubts.
  2. Practise mathematics and language questions appropriate to the target class.
  3. Work on reasoning and general-awareness topics included in the current syllabus.
  4. Attempt a short timed test and review incorrect answers.
  5. Revisit weak topics and keep a record of progress.

What Parents Can Do at Home

Parents can help by setting a predictable study time, providing a quiet place to work, checking that homework is completed and encouraging the child to explain how an answer was reached. Keep practice balanced with sleep, play and school responsibilities. If an answer is wrong, use it to identify the concept that needs revision rather than focusing only on the score.
