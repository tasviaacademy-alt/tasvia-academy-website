JNVST 2027 Arithmetic: A 30-Minute Maths Strategy with Worked Examples

Help your child approach the Arithmetic Test calmly and accurately. This guide turns the official NVS topic list into a clear revision route, shows how to practise within the recommended 30 minutes and includes original questions with explanations.

By TASVIA Academy  ·  Published 16 November 2026  ·  8 min read
✦ Ask about Navodaya preparation Call 78421 38739 Open Practice Zone

The Arithmetic Test rewards clear concepts more than hurried guessing. A child who understands place value, operations, fractions, measurement and simple data can solve many questions with a calm, repeatable method. The goal of the final days is not to learn every trick; it is to reduce avoidable mistakes and use the available time sensibly.

Important date: The NVS prospectus schedules JNVST 2027 Class VI for Saturday, 28 November 2026, 11:30 AM–1:30 PM. The official portal currently says admit cards are live for registered candidates. This guide is for students who have already registered; the published application deadline was 31 July 2026.

Arithmetic topics listed in the official prospectus

Use the following list as a checklist. The prospectus emphasises understanding and application of concepts, so practise questions that require choosing the right operation—not only memorising a formula.

1. Number and numeric system

Ascending and descending order, nearest numbers, number names, place value and value of digits, including 10, 100 and 1,000.

2. Four fundamental operations

Addition, subtraction, multiplication and division of whole numbers. Practise estimation to check whether an answer is reasonable.

3. Factors and multiples

Factors, multiples and their properties. Review prime numbers and simple prime factorisation.

4. Fractions

Addition and subtraction of like fractions and multiplication of fractions. The prospectus specifically excludes unlike-fraction addition/subtraction and division of fractional numbers.

5. Measurement

Length, mass, capacity, time, money and unit conversions. Write the unit beside every answer.

6. Simplification

Evaluate numerical expressions carefully, following the correct order of operations and checking each step.

7. Perimeter and area

Perimeter of polygons; area of a square, rectangle and triangle as a part of a rectangle.

8. Angles, directions and mapping

Recognise basic angle types and apply them to simple direction and map questions.

9. Data analysis

Read and compare information shown in a bar diagram, table or pictograph. Check the scale before calculating.

The prospectus describes the section as covering 12 topics, then presents the coverage under nine numbered headings with grouped sub-areas. This guide follows the headings and subtopics actually shown in the PDF rather than inventing extra labels. The official PDF remains the final authority.

A reliable four-step method for word problems

  1. Read twice: identify what the question asks, not just the numbers it contains.
  2. Mark the information: note the quantities, units and relationship—total, difference, equal groups, remaining amount or measurement.
  3. Choose the operation: decide whether addition, subtraction, multiplication, division or a formula is needed. Do not choose an operation from one keyword alone.
  4. Check: estimate the answer and confirm the unit. For a perimeter, the answer is a length; for area, it is in square units.

Worked examples to practise the method

Example 1 — Whole-number subtraction

A library has 4,825 books. It lends 1,976 books. How many remain?

Working: 4,825 − 1,976 = 2,849 books. Check by adding 2,849 + 1,976 = 4,825.

Example 2 — Like fractions

A child reads 3/8 of a story in the morning and 2/8 in the evening. What fraction has been read?

Working: The denominators are the same, so add the numerators: 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8.

Example 3 — Capacity conversion

A bottle contains 2.4 litres of water. How many millilitres is that?

Working: 1 litre = 1,000 millilitres. Therefore 2.4 × 1,000 = 2,400 mL.

Example 4 — Perimeter and area

A rectangular garden is 12 m long and 7 m wide. Find its perimeter and area.

Perimeter: 2 × (12 + 7) = 38 m.
Area: 12 × 7 = 84 m². Do not interchange the two units.

Example 5 — Simplification

Simplify: 18 + 6 ÷ 3 × 4.

Working: Division and multiplication are completed from left to right: 6 ÷ 3 = 2; 2 × 4 = 8; 18 + 8 = 26.

Example 6 — Reading a table

A student reads 12 pages on Monday, 15 on Tuesday and 9 on Wednesday. How many pages are read altogether?

Working: 12 + 15 + 9 = 36 pages. In data questions, read every row and the unit before calculating.

How to use the 30 minutes

The NVS prospectus suggests 30 minutes for Arithmetic. It does not prescribe a fixed number of minutes for each question. Since 20 questions are set for 30 minutes, the average available time is 90 seconds per question. The following is a TASVIA practice strategy, not an additional NVS rule:

Practice windowSuggested action
First 3 minutesScan the section. Begin with familiar, direct questions.
Next 18 minutesSolve the main set. Keep calculations neat and avoid spending too long on one item.
Next 5 minutesReturn to questions marked for review and check units or arithmetic.
Final 4 minutesCheck the question numbers and OMR bubbles. Ensure each chosen answer is marked clearly.
Practice rule: If a question is taking too long, put a small mark beside it in the question booklet and move on. Return later if time permits. Do not make stray marks or attempt to erase an OMR response.

12-day Arithmetic revision plan: 16–27 November 2026

Keep the daily session short and focused. A suggested 35–45 minute routine is 5 minutes of mental calculation, 20 minutes on the day's topic, 10–15 minutes of timed questions and 5 minutes to correct mistakes.

Day 1 · 16 Nov

Number sense

Place value, number names, ascending and descending order, nearest 10/100/1,000.

Day 2 · 17 Nov

Four operations

Practise addition, subtraction, multiplication and division with checking.

Day 3 · 18 Nov

Factors and multiples

List factors, find common multiples and revise prime factorisation.

Day 4 · 19 Nov

Fractions

Work on like-fraction addition and subtraction and multiplication of fractions.

Day 5 · 20 Nov

Measurement

Convert length, mass and capacity units. Write the correct unit in every answer.

Day 6 · 21 Nov

Time and money

Practise clock time, elapsed time, simple money calculations and conversions.

Day 7 · 22 Nov

Simplification

Evaluate numerical expressions step by step; check the order of operations.

Day 8 · 23 Nov

Perimeter and area

Draw squares and rectangles, label sides and distinguish length units from square units.

Day 9 · 24 Nov

Angles and directions

Identify basic angles and practise simple direction and map questions.

Day 10 · 25 Nov

Tables and pictographs

Read a table, bar diagram and pictograph; check labels and scale before answering.

Day 11 · 26 Nov

One timed set

Attempt 20 mixed Arithmetic questions in 30 minutes, then analyse every error.

Day 12 · 27 Nov

Light revision

Review the error notebook and key conversions. Avoid a late-night full mock test.

What parents can do this week

  • Keep one fixed, quiet practice slot and a simple checklist. Avoid adding several new books or worksheets at the last moment.
  • Ask the child to explain how an answer was found. Explaining the method often reveals a misunderstanding that a score alone cannot show.
  • Maintain a small error notebook with three columns: question type, what went wrong and the corrected method.
  • Use everyday examples—shopping bills, water bottles, travel time and room measurements—to make units and arithmetic familiar.
  • Protect sleep, meals and outdoor breaks. Confidence and alertness matter as much as one more worksheet.

Eligibility and admit-card reminder for parents

The 2027–28 Class VI prospectus specifies a date-of-birth range of 1 May 2015 to 31 July 2017, both dates inclusive. A candidate must be studying in Class V for the full 2026–27 academic session in a recognised school and must meet the district-residence and previous-schooling requirements, including full academic sessions in Classes III, IV and V. The selection is district-specific. A candidate who has already passed Class V in an earlier session or has appeared in JNVST before is not eligible under the prospectus.

The application deadline in the prospectus was 31 July 2026. The NVS portal now states that Class VI JNVST 2027 admit cards are live for registered candidates. Parents should download the admit card using the candidate's registration details and verify the child's name, photograph, language and allotted centre.

Exam-day instructions students must remember

Parent and student checklist

  • Download and print the official admit card. Check the examination centre and the candidate's details in advance.
  • Reach early. The prospectus says candidates must be seated in the allotted room by 11:00 AM; late arrivals are not permitted.
  • The scheduled test time is 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM. Follow the centre instructions printed on the admit card.
  • Carry the identity/residence document specified by NVS: the prospectus refers to the candidate's Aadhaar card or the government-approved parent residence certificate uploaded in the application.
  • Use only a blue or black ball-point pen on the OMR sheet. Pencil is prohibited.
  • Darken one selected circle neatly. Do not erase, strike through, use whitener or change a darkened response.
  • Check that the OMR sheet and question booklet have the same number. Tell the invigilator immediately if they do not match.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are in the JNVST 2027 Arithmetic Test?

The NVS prospectus lists 20 objective questions for 25 marks, with a suggested duration of 30 minutes.

Is there negative marking?

No. The prospectus states that each correct answer carries 1.25 marks and there is no negative marking. Students should still read every question carefully and mark the OMR sheet accurately.

Which Arithmetic topics should a child revise?

Revise number and numeric system, the four operations, factors and multiples, fractions, measurement and unit conversion, simplification, perimeter and area, angles with directions and mapping, and data analysis using bar diagrams, tables and pictographs.

When is the JNVST 2027 Class VI exam?

The official NVS prospectus schedules it for 28 November 2026, from 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM. Candidates must be seated by 11:00 AM.

Can a student submit a fresh application now?

The prospectus lists 31 July 2026 as the last date to apply. This article is for candidates who have already registered. Families should rely on the NVS portal for any official notice; TASVIA cannot extend or alter NVS application dates.

Are the practice questions on this page official NVS questions?

No. They are original TASVIA revision questions written to practise the concepts listed in the prospectus. For official sample questions, use the NVS admission website and prospectus.
