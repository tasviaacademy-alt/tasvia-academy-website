Daily General Knowledge Practice for Navodaya and Sainik School Aspirants

General knowledge grows through curiosity and regular reading. Instead of asking children to memorise long lists, parents can help them build a daily habit of noticing, understanding and recalling useful information.

By TASVIA Academy  ·  Updated 30 October 2026
Important: Admission eligibility, syllabus, dates and test instructions may change by admission year. Always verify the current official prospectus or information bulletin before applying.

1. Keep GK age-appropriate and connected to life

Start with the student's surroundings: the state and country, important places, plants and animals, weather, health, transport, sports, science in everyday life and public institutions. Introduce current events in simple language and explain why they matter.

2. Use a 15-minute daily routine

  1. Read one short, reliable news or knowledge item.
  2. Discuss three facts in the child's own words.
  3. Ask five short questions from current and previously learned topics.
  4. Review incorrect answers without pressure.
  5. Record one new fact in a GK notebook.

3. Rotate topics across the week

Choose a theme for each day, such as India and Telangana, geography, science and environment, history and culture, sports, or important people and institutions. A topic rotation prevents practice from becoming random and helps children connect related facts.

4. Teach understanding, not just one-word answers

If a student learns the name of a river, also locate it on a map. If they learn about a scientific discovery, discuss the everyday problem it helps solve. Ask simple follow-up questions: where, why, how and what changed? This makes recall more meaningful.

5. Use quizzes as feedback

Short quizzes are useful when they include a mix of new and older questions. Keep a record of topics that need revision. Avoid treating every quiz as a competition; the purpose is to improve knowledge and confidence.

6. Choose reliable information sources

For current affairs and official facts, use credible news and government sources. Check dates and context, because information can change. For exam preparation, use the GK scope and instructions in the current JNVST or AISSEE prospectus rather than assuming every trending topic is examinable.

Frequently asked questions

How many GK questions should a child practise daily?

A small set of five to ten well-explained questions can be a useful starting point. Adjust the number to the child's age and learning level.

Should children memorise current affairs months in advance?

They should understand key events and practise recall, but avoid overwhelming them with long lists. Follow the exam's current scope.

Can parents help without being subject experts?

Yes. Read together, discuss everyday observations, use a map and encourage the child to explain what they learned.
