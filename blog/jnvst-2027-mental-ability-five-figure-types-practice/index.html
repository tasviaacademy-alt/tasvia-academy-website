JNVST 2027 Mental Ability Test: Five Figure Types, Solving Tips & Practice

Understand all five official figure-based MAT question types, learn a simple observation method and practise with original questions before the JNVST 2027 Class VI examination.

By TASVIA Academy  ·  Published 23 November 2026  ·  9 min read
✦ Ask about Navodaya MAT practice Call 78421 38739 Open Practice Zone

The Mental Ability Test (MAT) asks children to notice how shapes, lines and symbols fit together. A calm student does not need to guess quickly: first observe the figure, identify the rule, then compare the answer choices. This guide breaks the official JNVST 2027 MAT topics into five clear groups and gives an original mini-practice set for the final days before the examination.

For registered candidates: NVS schedules JNVST 2027 Class VI for Saturday, 28 November 2026, 11:30 AM–1:30 PM. Candidates must be seated by 11:00 AM. The NVS portal currently lists the admit-card download facility. Applications closed on 31 July 2026, so this article is a preparation guide—not an application notice.

JNVST 2027 MAT at a glance

20MAT questions
25Maximum marks
5Official figure-based parts
1.25Marks per correct answer

The prospectus describes MAT as a non-verbal test based on figures and diagrams. It is combined with Environmental Studies (EVS) in the first section: MAT has 20 questions for 25 marks and EVS has 20 questions for 25 marks, with 60 minutes suggested for the combined section. The full test has 80 objective questions for 100 marks over two hours. There is no negative marking.

Important timing point: NVS gives 60 minutes for MAT and EVS together; it does not prescribe a separate official MAT-only timer. Practise the two subjects as a combined section when doing a full mock.

The five MAT question types in the NVS prospectus

These are the five headings used in the official prospectus. Prepare all five rather than assuming that one type will dominate.

Official MAT partWhat the student seesFirst thing to check
I. Pattern CompletionA figure with a missing portion; the prospectus also mentions alphabetical and number patterns under this heading.Repeated lines, shapes, direction and the missing section.
II. Figure Series CompletionThree figures in a sequence and a blank space for the next figure.What is added, removed, rotated or moved each time.
III. Geometrical Figure CompletionA geometrical figure or part that must be completed, using shapes such as triangles, squares and circles.Which edges and curves must join without changing the outline.
IV. Mirror and Water ImagingA figure and its reflected image across a mirror or water line.Whether the reflection reverses left–right or top–bottom.
V. Embedded FigureA small target figure that is hidden inside one of the larger answer figures.Trace the target's angles and line connections, not its size.
Read the prospectus carefully: it says MAT has five parts and 20 questions in total. Its sample-question ranges are shown as 1–4, 5–8, 9–11, 12–16 and 17–20, which are not evenly sized. Do not assume a guaranteed question count for each type; practise all five.

A simple method for solving figure questions

  1. Look at the whole figure first. Notice the outer boundary, centre line, corners and any missing space.
  2. Track one feature at a time. Count lines, dots, corners or shaded areas before checking several features together.
  3. Check direction. A shape may rotate, flip, move clockwise or move from one corner to another.
  4. Use elimination. Remove options that have the wrong number of lines, wrong orientation or an extra part.
  5. Confirm the fit. For a missing piece, imagine joining the edges. For an embedded figure, trace the target without changing its angles.

Ask the child to explain the rule in one short sentence: “one dot is added each time” or “the arrow flips left to right.” Explaining the rule is a useful way to check that the answer is not a lucky guess.

How to approach each of the five types

1. Pattern completion

Compare the missing space with the parts already shown. Look for a line that continues smoothly, a repeated curve, matching corners or a pattern that alternates. Check the direction of every diagonal before selecting a piece. If the pattern contains letters or numbers, look for a simple order or repetition rather than trying several rules at once.

2. Figure series completion

Compare figure one with figure two, then figure two with figure three. Ask: did a line appear, did a dot move, did the shape rotate, or did one feature change position? Apply the same single rule to find the next figure.

3. Geometrical figure completion

Focus on the outer boundary first. A correct piece should join the open edges and preserve the intended triangle, square or circle. Do not choose a piece just because it has a similar shape; its edges must connect in the correct places.

4. Mirror and water imaging

A vertical mirror reverses left and right. A water image across a horizontal line reverses top and bottom. Before choosing an option, imagine the reflection line and check the direction of any arrow, letter-like mark or uneven feature. A common mistake is to rotate the figure instead of reflecting it.

5. Embedded figures

Ignore colour and size unless the question makes them relevant. Trace the target figure's line path through the larger option. The hidden shape may be part of a more complex drawing, but its angles and connections must remain the same.

Five original MAT practice questions

Try each question before reading the explanation. These are original TASVIA practice questions created to practise the official question styles; they are not copied from an NVS paper and are not predictions of the actual examination.

Q1 · PATTERN COMPLETION

Which symbol comes next?

▲ ○ ▲ ○ ?
A. ▲B. ○C. ■D. ◆

Answer: A — ▲. The two symbols alternate: triangle, circle, triangle, circle. The next symbol is a triangle.

Q2 · FIGURE SERIES

Complete the growing series.

● | ●● | ●●● | ?
A. ●●B. ●●●●C. ●D. ●●●●●

Answer: B — four dots. One dot is added at each step, so the fourth figure contains four dots.

Q3 · GEOMETRICAL COMPLETION

Which line closes the triangle?

/\ / \ ?
A. Horizontal lineB. Vertical lineC. Curved arcD. Short diagonal

Answer: A — horizontal line. The two sloping sides meet at the top. A horizontal base joins their open lower endpoints to complete the triangle.

Q4 · MIRROR IMAGE

What is the vertical mirror image of ↗?

Problem: ↗ | Mirror: │
A. ↖B. ↘C. ↗D. ↙

Answer: A — ↖. A vertical mirror reverses left and right. A water image across a horizontal line would instead turn ↗ into ↘.

Q5 · EMBEDDED FIGURE

The target is a lower-left corner: └. Which option contains it?

Target: └
A. ┌─┐B. ┘─┐C. └─┐D. ┐─┘

Answer: C. Option C contains the same lower-left corner with the vertical and horizontal lines meeting in the same direction.

Five-day MAT revision plan: 23–27 November

Use short, focused practice blocks. This is a suggested TASVIA revision plan, not an NVS-prescribed timetable.

23 November

Pattern completion

Practise missing parts, alternating shapes, line direction and simple number or letter patterns shown in figures.

24 November

Figure series

Complete sequences by tracking one change at a time: add, remove, rotate or move.

25 November

Geometrical completion

Revise triangle, square and circle outlines. Check that edges and curves meet correctly.

26 November

Reflections and embedded figures

Practise vertical mirror, horizontal water images and tracing a small figure inside a larger one.

27 November

Mixed MAT review

Attempt a 20-question mixed MAT set, review every mistake and stop heavy practice early enough to rest.

For a full mock, follow the official combined MAT + EVS section duration of 60 minutes. Do not treat a separate MAT-only time split as an official rule.

How parents can help without increasing exam stress

  • Keep a pencil-and-paper practice sheet ready and ask the child to point out the rule before choosing an option.
  • Review mistakes by type: direction, counting, missing line, reflection or hidden shape. Practise the weak type again the next day.
  • Use a few accurate questions rather than forcing long sessions. Praise careful observation and clear explanations.
  • Keep the final evening light. Arrange travel, admit card and permitted identification documents in advance.
  • Do not promise a selection result based on one mock score. Selection depends on the official process and applicable criteria.

JNVST 2027 eligibility reminder

This guide is for families of candidates who have already registered. The official prospectus states that a Class VI candidate must be a bona fide resident of the district for which admission is sought and be studying in Class V during the 2026–27 academic session in a recognised school. The age range is 1 May 2015 to 31 July 2017, both dates inclusive. The prospectus also sets conditions for full-session study in Classes III, IV and V and district/school location. Check the original prospectus for the full rules; a child should not rely on age alone to decide eligibility.

Exam-day checklist from the NVS prospectus

Before leaving home

  • Download and carry the candidate's official admit card.
  • Carry the candidate's Aadhaar card or the Government-approved residence certificate uploaded in the application portal, as permitted by NVS.
  • Carry the candidate's own blue or black ballpoint pen. NVS says pencil use is prohibited for the OMR sheet.
  • Check the allotted examination centre and travel route the previous day.
Reporting time: The examination is scheduled from 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM. Candidates must be seated in the allotted room by 11:00 AM; late entry is not permitted.

At the centre, check that the number printed on the OMR sheet matches the question booklet number and tell the invigilator immediately if it does not. Darken only the chosen circle carefully. The prospectus says changes to a darkened circle, striking, correction fluid and erasing are not allowed; such responses will not be evaluated.

Frequently asked questions

1. How many Mental Ability questions are in JNVST 2027?

The official prospectus lists 20 MAT questions for 25 marks. Each correct answer carries 1.25 marks and there is no negative marking.

2. What are the five JNVST MAT question types?

Pattern completion, figure series completion, geometrical figure completion, mirror and water imaging, and embedded figures.

3. Is the JNVST Mental Ability Test verbal or non-verbal?

NVS describes it as a non-verbal test based on figures and diagrams. Pattern completion also mentions alphabetical and number patterns in the prospectus.

4. Does MAT have a separate official time limit?

No separate MAT-only time is stated. MAT and EVS together form the first section, for which the prospectus suggests 60 minutes.

5. When is the JNVST 2027 Class VI examination?

The official date is 28 November 2026, from 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM. Candidates must be seated by 11:00 AM.

6. What should a candidate bring for the OMR test?

Carry the admit card, the identification document permitted by NVS, and a blue or black ballpoint pen. Pencil is prohibited for the OMR sheet; follow the admit-card and prospectus instructions.
