AISSEE 2027 Class VI: 150-Minute Mock Test & OMR Accuracy Plan

1. Understand the AISSEE 2027 Class VI paper

The Class VI paper is an objective, multiple-choice examination conducted in paper-and-pencil mode using an OMR answer sheet. The NTA bulletin lists 125 questions for 300 marks and a total duration of 150 minutes (2 hours 30 minutes).

SectionQuestionsMarks per correct answerTotal marks
Language25250
Mathematics503150
Intelligence25250
General Knowledge25250
Total125—300

Mathematics contributes half of the total marks, so it deserves steady daily practice. But the other three sections together contribute the remaining 150 marks. A balanced plan is safer than ignoring any section.

Two official facts to remember: the examination is OMR-based, and the bulletin states that there is no negative marking for a wrong response. The bulletin does not prescribe separate section-wise time limits.

2. A practical 150-minute mock-test split

Use this as a starting point during practice. It is a suggested TASVIA time-management plan, not an official NTA timetable. Adjust it after reviewing the student's mock-test accuracy and speed.

Practice blockSuggested timeWhat to focus on
Language · 25 questions25 minutesRead each question carefully; avoid spending too long on one vocabulary or grammar item.
Mathematics · 50 questions65 minutesGive time to calculations and word problems; mark difficult questions for a later return.
Intelligence · 25 questions25 minutesLook for the rule or relationship before selecting an option.
General Knowledge · 25 questions20 minutesAnswer known facts confidently and avoid repeatedly changing a considered response.
Final OMR and response check15 minutesCheck question-number alignment, missed responses and any instructions given by the invigilator.
Total150 minutesFull-length Class VI practice

Do not leave all OMR marking until the final 15 minutes. During a mock, transfer answers in short, controlled blocks while keeping the question number and OMR row aligned. Use the final review period to find omissions or numbering slips. In the actual examination, follow the invigilator's directions and the instructions printed on the supplied answer sheet.

Simple time rule: if a question is taking unusually long, make a note on the question booklet only if permitted by the examination instructions, move forward and return later. Never allow one difficult problem to consume the time needed for several easier questions.

3. Build accurate OMR habits before exam day

OMR practice is not only about knowing the answer. It is also about keeping the response number matched to the correct row. Practise with a sample sheet during mock tests so the process feels familiar.

Check the question number first.

Before recording a response, compare the question number in the booklet with the corresponding row on the practice OMR sheet.

Work in short batches.

After a small group of questions, pause briefly and confirm that no response has shifted to the next or previous row.

Keep unanswered questions visible.

Use a simple marker on your practice question booklet to revisit uncertain items. Do not create extra marks or notes on the actual OMR sheet unless specifically instructed.

Review the response sequence.

During the final check, compare the last attempted question number with the last recorded OMR row and scan for accidental gaps.

Follow the centre's instructions.

The NTA bulletin says pens will be provided by NTA. Follow the invigilator's directions for filling the actual OMR sheet; do not assume a coaching mock's stationery rules apply at the centre.

After every mock, record three things: the number of questions attempted, the number answered correctly and the number of OMR mismatches or blank rows. The third number should move towards zero with repeated practice.

4. What to revise from the official Class VI syllabus

The AISSEE-2027 bulletin places the Class VI topic list in Appendix II. Use the appendix as the master checklist. These grouped reminders are a quick revision map, not a replacement for the complete official list.

Mathematics · 50 questions / 150 marksGive this section regular practice

Natural numbers, LCM and HCF, unitary method, fractions, ratio, profit and loss, simplification, averages, percentages, area and perimeter, simple interest, angles, units, Roman numerals, decimals, speed and time, temperature and number operations.

Intelligence · 25 questions / 50 marksPractise visual and logical thinking

Analogies, spatial and mathematical patterns, classification, visual and logical reasoning, series and sequences, critical thinking, problem solving and family relationships.

Language · 25 questions / 50 marksRead and understand accurately

Comprehension, vocabulary, prepositions, articles, verbs, confusing words, question tags, sentence types, tenses, nouns, pronouns, spelling, sentence order, synonyms and antonyms.

General Knowledge · 25 questions / 50 marksConnect facts to everyday life

Indian symbols and culture, geography, awards and personalities, defence, sports, organisations, the solar system, climate, water, energy, farming, food, health, plants, animals and scientific devices.

For the exact topic wording, use Appendix II of the official AISSEE-2027 bulletin. The bulletin also lists the earlier question papers as available through the NTA website. Practise from current, age-appropriate material and review mistakes instead of memorising unverified “important question” lists.

5. A seven-day practice cycle for Class VI

Repeat this weekly cycle and gradually increase the amount of timed work as the student becomes more comfortable. Keep the workload realistic alongside school and rest.

Day 1 · MathematicsOne timed 50-question set

Review calculations, units, fractions and word problems. Write down the reason behind every wrong answer.

Day 2 · Language25 questions + reading

Practise one comprehension passage and revise grammar or vocabulary errors.

Day 3 · Intelligence25-question set

Rotate analogies, patterns, classification, series and logical reasoning.

Day 4 · General Knowledge25-question set

Revise one or two Appendix II themes and explain key facts aloud in the student's own words.

Day 5 · OMR accuracyShort mixed practice

Practise transferring answers in sequence and checking question-number alignment without rushing.

Day 6 · Full mock150 minutes

Attempt a complete Class VI paper under quiet, timed conditions and use a practice OMR sheet.

Day 7 · Review and recoverError notebook + light revision

Rework incorrect questions, identify one improvement target and allow time for rest and outdoor activity.

6. Class VI eligibility and dates to keep visible

For admission to the 2027–28 academic year, the AISSEE-2027 bulletin says a Class VI candidate must be 10 to 12 years old as on 31 March 2027, with date of birth from 1 April 2015 to 31 March 2017, both dates inclusive. Girls may apply for Class VI; parents should check the school-wise availability of seats in Appendix VI-A.

Correction window7–9 December 2026Up to 11:50 PM on 9 December
AISSEE 2027 examination31 January 2027Sunday · Class VI: 2:00 PM–4:30 PM

Class VI candidates can choose any of the 13 listed question-paper languages, including Telugu. The bulletin says the medium selected in the application cannot be changed after the option is exercised. Confirm the selected medium and the target school before submitting the form.

7. NTA exam-day instructions parents should know

The current bulletin contains a specific checklist for parents and guardians. Read it again with the admit card when it becomes available, because the admit card's reporting details and any later NTA notice must be followed.

  • Reach on time: the bulletin says candidates will not be allowed to enter after 1:30 PM. The examination is scheduled from 2:00 PM to 4:30 PM. Plan travel using the centre address and reporting time printed on the admit card.
  • Carry the required documents: the admit card with its self-declaration, passport-size photograph and thumb impression affixed as instructed; and a valid original photo ID proof.
  • Water: one personal transparent water bottle is listed among the permitted items.
  • Extra photograph: carry one passport-size photograph for the attendance sheet.
  • Diabetes-related provision: the bulletin permits sugar tablets or fruit for candidates with diabetes.
  • Do not bring stationery: the bulletin lists pens, scales, pencil boxes, writing pads, calculators, phones, watches and other items among barred items. It states that pens will be provided by NTA.
  • Follow the centre rules: candidates cannot leave the examination hall until the examination concludes and should not arrive on an empty stomach.
Important: Do not pack a pencil box or personal pen out of habit. Check the latest admit-card instructions and the NTA bulletin before leaving home. The centre will not provide storage for barred belongings.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are there in AISSEE 2027 Class VI?

The NTA bulletin lists 125 MCQs: 25 Language, 50 Mathematics, 25 Intelligence and 25 General Knowledge. The paper carries 300 marks and lasts 150 minutes.

Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?

No. The AISSEE-2027 Information Bulletin states that there is no negative marking for a wrong response.

Can a Class VI candidate choose Telugu as the examination medium?

Yes. Telugu is one of the 13 listed Class VI question-paper languages. The selected medium cannot be changed after the option is exercised in the application form.

What is the age eligibility for AISSEE 2027 Class VI?

The candidate should be 10 to 12 years old as on 31 March 2027, with date of birth from 1 April 2015 to 31 March 2017, both dates inclusive.

Can students bring their own pen or stationery to the exam centre?

The bulletin says NTA will provide pens and lists stationery, including pens, pencil boxes and writing pads, among barred items. Follow the current admit-card and centre instructions.

When should a Class VI candidate reach the AISSEE 2027 centre?

The bulletin says entry will not be allowed after 1:30 PM. The examination is scheduled from 2:00 PM to 4:30 PM. Follow the reporting time printed on the candidate's admit card and reach well in advance.
