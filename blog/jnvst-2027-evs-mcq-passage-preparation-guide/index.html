1. JNVST 2027 Class VI: exam essentials

The official NVS prospectus schedules the Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalaya Selection Test (JNVST) 2027 for admission to Class VI in the 2027–28 academic session. The following details are taken from the current prospectus and NVS admission portal.

ItemOfficial detail
Examination dateSaturday, 28 November 2026
Test time11:30 AM to 1:30 PM
Seating deadlineBy 11:00 AM; latecomers will not be permitted
Whole paper80 objective questions · 100 marks · 2 hours
MAT + EVS combined block40 questions · 50 marks · 60 minutes
EVS component20 questions · 25 marks
Marking1.25 marks for each correct answer; no negative marking
Admit cardShown as available for registered candidates on the NVS portal on 9 November 2026

The 60-minute duration applies to the combined Mental Ability and EVS section. NVS does not prescribe a separate official time limit for EVS alone. The 10-day plan below is a TASVIA practice suggestion, not an official timetable.

2. How the EVS section is structured

The prospectus describes 20 EVS questions within the first section of the paper. It specifies 15 multiple-choice questions and one passage followed by five questions. This means students need both topic knowledge and the ability to read a short passage carefully.

EVS question typeNumberWhat to practise
Topic-based MCQs15Recall, classification, everyday science, simple processes and relationships
Passage-based questions5Main idea, stated details, sequence, vocabulary in context and simple inference
Total EVS2025 marks

Do not prepare EVS as a list of isolated facts only. The NVS prospectus says the subject takes an interdisciplinary approach, bringing together basic Science, Social Science and Environmental Education to assess awareness of the child's surroundings.

3. Class VI eligibility: a quick parent check

Although this is a preparation article, families should keep the key eligibility conditions in view. The official prospectus states that the candidate must:

  • Have been born between 1 May 2015 and 31 July 2017, both dates inclusive.
  • Be studying in Class V for the full 2026–27 academic session in a Government, Government-aided or other recognised school, subject to the prospectus rules.
  • Have studied and passed Classes III, IV and V in recognised schooling, spending one full academic session in each class, as required by the prospectus.
  • Meet the district-specific bona fide residence and Class V study conditions for the JNV applied to. A limited neighbouring-district school exception exists only under the conditions and verification described in clause 4.1.
Important: Eligibility is not decided by age alone. Parents should read clauses 4.1–4.5 and the relevant district/block annexure in the official JNVST 2027 prospectus. Do not rely on an old year's age chart or a social-media post.

4. Official EVS topic areas to revise

The NVS prospectus groups EVS into four broad learning areas. Use these as a checklist, then practise explaining each idea in the child's own words.

A. The natural world

  • Transportation, rivers and mountains.
  • Plants and animals living on land and in water.
  • Natural disasters and different types of houses or shelters.
  • The water cycle and related everyday observations.

B. The human body

  • Food, nutrients, hygiene and cleanliness.
  • Super senses and how people or animals use their senses.
  • Basic knowledge of the digestive, circulatory and respiratory systems.

C. Science in daily life

  • Food preservation methods.
  • Water and air pollution.
  • Conservation of water and soil.

D. Social surroundings

  • India-related superlatives, States and capitals, and national symbols.
  • Landscapes, festivals and seasons.
  • Forests, crops, clothes and fibres.

These headings follow the topic groups in the current NVS prospectus. The examples below are original TASVIA practice material, not questions copied from an actual examination paper.

5. How to approach the 15 EVS MCQs

STEP 1

Understand the idea

Ask what a process does and why it happens. For example, explain evaporation, condensation and rainfall in the correct order rather than memorising only the words.

STEP 2

Sort and compare

Group plants, animals, materials, foods, transport and natural features. Practise identifying the one option that does not belong.

STEP 3

Connect to daily life

Discuss safe drinking water, food storage, cleanliness, pollution and conservation using familiar home, school and neighbourhood examples.

STEP 4

Explain the answer

After choosing an option, ask the student to say why it is correct and why one tempting alternative is wrong. This builds understanding and reduces guessing.

For every practice set, keep a small error log with three columns: question/topic, why the answer was missed and the corrected idea. Revisit the log after two days. A short explanation remembered well is more useful than copying a page of notes.

6. A step-by-step method for the EVS passage

The official pattern includes one EVS passage followed by five questions. A child can use this simple routine:

  1. Read the full passage once. First understand who or what it is about. Do not stop at every unfamiliar word.
  2. Notice key details. Look for names, places, numbers, reasons, results and words such as first, next, because and therefore.
  3. Read each question carefully. Identify whether it asks for a stated fact, the main idea, a sequence, a reason or a word meaning.
  4. Return to the relevant sentence. Find evidence in the passage before selecting an option. Avoid adding outside information that the passage does not provide.
  5. Use context for vocabulary. Read the sentence before and after an unfamiliar word and choose the meaning that fits the passage.
  6. Check the question number and OMR row. Keep the response aligned with the correct question.
Teaching tip: After a passage, ask the student to retell it in two sentences. If the child can explain the main idea and sequence without looking, comprehension is improving.

7. Original EVS passage practice

Read the passage and answer the five questions.

Rainwater for the neighbourhood

Before the monsoon, the residents of a small neighbourhood cleaned the channels that carried rainwater from rooftops to a storage tank. A mesh at the pipe opening stopped leaves from entering. When the first rain washed dust from the roofs, the residents directed that water away from the tank. Later rainwater flowed into the tank and was stored for gardening and cleaning. The drinking water used by families was treated separately. Each week, a volunteer checked the tank level and reported leaks. The residents repaired dripping taps and used buckets for outdoor cleaning so that stored water would last longer.

  1. What was the main purpose of the storage tank?
    A. To store rainwater for later use   B. To grow fish   C. To wash rooftops immediately   D. To collect leaves
  2. What did the mesh at the pipe opening stop?
    A. Sunlight   B. Leaves   C. Rain clouds   D. Sand from the garden
  3. Why was the first rainwater directed away from the tank?
    A. It was too cold   B. It had washed dust from the roofs   C. The tank was full   D. The pipes were removed
  4. Which action helped the stored water last longer?
    A. Leaving taps open   B. Washing roads with a hose   C. Repairing dripping taps   D. Removing the mesh
  5. In the passage, “directed away” most nearly means:
    A. Redirected   B. Heated   C. Counted   D. Covered

Answer key with reasons

  • 1 — A: The passage says rainwater was stored for gardening and cleaning.
  • 2 — B: The mesh stopped leaves from entering the pipe.
  • 3 — B: The first rain had washed dust from the rooftops.
  • 4 — C: Repairing dripping taps reduced water loss.
  • 5 — A: “Directed away” means the water was sent in another direction.

8. A focused 10-day EVS practice plan

This is a suggested TASVIA revision sequence for 9–18 November 2026. It is not an NVS timetable. Keep sessions short, include breaks and adjust the pace to the child's school routine.

DayFocusPractice task
1Natural world IRivers, mountains, transport and water cycle; draw one simple process.
2Natural world IIPlants, animals, habitats, shelters and natural disasters; make comparison cards.
3Human body IFood, nutrients, hygiene and cleanliness; explain everyday examples.
4Human body IISuper senses and basic body systems; use labelled diagrams for recall.
5Science in daily lifeFood preservation, water pollution and air pollution; identify causes and prevention.
6ConservationWater and soil conservation; practise cause-and-effect questions.
7Social surroundings IStates, capitals, national symbols and landscapes; use a simple map.
8Social surroundings IIFestivals, seasons, forests, crops, clothes and fibres; complete a short recall quiz.
9Mixed EVS practiceAttempt 15 topic MCQs and one five-question passage; review every error.
10Recall and correctionRetest weak topics, explain answers aloud and update the mistake notebook.

After this cycle, alternate short EVS revision with Mental Ability practice and attempt the full combined MAT–EVS block within the official 60-minute duration. Do not treat 30 minutes as an official EVS-only limit; the prospectus gives the combined timing.

9. How parents can help without adding pressure

  • Spend 10 minutes discussing one everyday topic: rain, food storage, plants, transport, hygiene or water use.
  • Ask the child to explain an idea in their own words instead of memorising a long answer.
  • Use a map, a simple drawing or objects at home to make concepts visible.
  • Review incorrect answers calmly. Praise careful reasoning and improvement, not only the score.
  • Keep the admit card, centre details and permitted stationery ready. Avoid sharing the child's registration number or admit card publicly.
  • Protect sleep, meals, play and schoolwork. A calm routine is more helpful than a last-minute overload.

10. Important official examination instructions

  • The candidate must carry the proper admit card; the prospectus says no candidate will be allowed to appear without it.
  • Students must be seated in the allotted room by 11:00 AM. Latecomers will not be permitted.
  • Use only a blue or black ballpoint pen on the OMR sheet. Pencil use is prohibited.
  • Darken the selected circle clearly. The prospectus does not allow changing a darkened response, erasing it or using correction fluid.
  • Check that the OMR sheet and question booklet carry the same number/code; tell the invigilator immediately if they do not match.

The prospectus states that there is no negative marking. Students should still read every question carefully and mark answers neatly; a correctly understood question can be lost through an incorrectly marked OMR row.

Frequently asked questions

How many EVS questions are there in JNVST 2027?

The NVS prospectus specifies 20 EVS questions for 25 marks: 15 multiple-choice questions and one passage followed by five questions.

Is EVS given a separate time limit?

No separate EVS-only duration is stated. Mental Ability and EVS together form a 40-question, 50-mark block with 60 minutes in the official test pattern.

What topics are included in JNVST EVS?

The prospectus groups topics under the natural world, the human body, science in daily life and social surroundings. See the topic checklist above and the official prospectus for the full wording.

When is the JNVST 2027 Class VI examination?

The official prospectus schedules the test for Saturday, 28 November 2026, from 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM. Candidates must be seated by 11:00 AM.

Are JNVST 2027 admit cards available?

Yes. The NVS admission portal states that Class VI JNVST 2027 admit cards are live for registered candidates. Download them through the official NVS login page.

Is there negative marking in JNVST 2027?

No. The prospectus states that each correct answer carries 1.25 marks and there is no negative marking.

What is the age range for Class VI JNVST 2027?

The prospectus specifies a date of birth from 1 May 2015 to 31 July 2017, both dates inclusive. Schooling and district-residence conditions also apply.
