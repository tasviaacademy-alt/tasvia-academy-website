AISSEE 2027 Application Guide: Dates, Eligibility, Documents & a Practical Study Plan

1. AISSEE 2027 important dates

The National Testing Agency (NTA) published the AISSEE 2027 Information Bulletin for admission to existing Sainik Schools and approved New Sainik Schools for the 2027–28 academic year. The application window began on 3 November 2026. Keep the following dates visible and recheck the NTA portal for any later correction notice.

Regular application & fee deadline2 December 2026Up to 11:50 PM
Late application window3–6 December 2026Additional late fee: ₹500
Application correction window7–9 December 2026Up to 11:50 PM on 9 December
Advance exam-city intimation11 January 2027As scheduled in the bulletin
Admit card download25 January 2027From the NTA website
AISSEE 2027 examination31 January 2027Sunday · Class VI: 2–4:30 PM · Class IX: 2–5 PM

The regular application and fee-payment deadline is 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM. NTA has also listed a late-submission period from 3 to 6 December with an additional ₹500 late fee. Do not wait for the late window if the form and documents are ready. The result date is not specified in the current bulletin; check the NTA portal for later announcements.

2. Check age eligibility before starting the form

Age is calculated as on 31 March 2027. Confirm the date of birth against the child's official birth certificate before registering.

Admission classRequired age on 31 March 2027Date-of-birth rangeOther condition
Class VI10 to 12 years1 April 2015 to 31 March 2017 (inclusive)Girls may apply; check school-wise seat availability.
Class IX13 to 15 years1 April 2012 to 31 March 2014 (inclusive)Must have passed Class VIII from a recognised school by the time of admission. Girls' admission is subject to vacancies.

These are the age bands for the regular Sainik School entry routes. Approved New Sainik Schools also have specific admission routes. Under the Category B route, the candidate must already be studying in the same approved New Sainik School where admission is sought. Check the school's own admission policy and the relevant NTA bulletin section before selecting a route.

New Sainik School route: The bulletin describes a minimum 40% Category A route based on the All-India AISSEE merit list and an up-to-60% Category B route for eligible students already enrolled in that same approved New Sainik School. The two routes are not interchangeable; verify the school's status, vacancies and candidate eligibility in the official bulletin.

3. Apply through the official NTA portal

Use the official AISSEE portal only. NTA says the application is online and only one application should be submitted by a candidate for the relevant admission level.

Register with a working email address and mobile number

Use the candidate's or parent's active contact details. Keep access to them for NTA messages and later counselling.

Enter personal and school details carefully

Check the child's name, date of birth, class, category, domicile and selected schools against the original documents before saving.

Upload the required files

Prepare the photograph, signature, thumb impression and applicable certificates in the file formats and sizes specified by NTA.

Select the question-paper medium and exam-city preferences

Class VI candidates can select from the 13 listed languages, including Telugu. Select four convenient exam-city codes in priority order. NTA may allot another city if required.

Pay the examination fee and save the confirmation page

Payment alone is not enough: the application is complete only after the required steps are finished and the confirmation page is generated. Download and keep it safely.

Document and upload checklist

  • Candidate photograph in JPG format: 10 KB to 200 KB.
  • Candidate signature in JPG format: 10 KB to 50 KB.
  • Candidate's left-hand thumb impression in JPG format: 10 KB to 50 KB. The bulletin permits the right thumb if the left thumb is unavailable.
  • Date-of-birth certificate issued by the competent authority.
  • Domicile certificate and caste/community/category certificate, where applicable.
  • Defence service certificate or PPO for the relevant defence/ex-servicemen category, where applicable.
  • Certificate from the Principal if the child is currently studying in an approved New Sainik School and applying under the applicable route.
  • Certificates should be prepared as PDFs within the file-size range specified in the bulletin (50 KB to 300 KB).

The fee listed in the bulletin is ₹850 for General, wards of Defence personnel and ex-servicemen, and OBC-NCL candidates as per the Central List; it is ₹700 for SC/ST candidates. Applicable bank charges and taxes may be additional. The examination fee is non-refundable. State OBC lists are not automatically the same as the Central OBC-NCL list, so check the bulletin's category instructions carefully.

The examination is an offline paper-pencil test using OMR answer sheets and multiple-choice questions. The current bulletin states that there is no negative marking for a wrong response.

5. What should students revise?

The official syllabus is in Appendix II (Class VI) and Appendix III (Class IX) of the NTA Information Bulletin. Use those appendices as the final topic checklist rather than relying on an old coaching handout.

Class VI: focus areas

  • Mathematics: natural numbers, LCM and HCF, unitary method, fractions, ratio and proportion, profit and loss, simplification, averages, percentages, area and perimeter, simple interest, angles, unit conversion, Roman numerals, circles, volume, decimals, speed and time, and operations on numbers.
  • Intelligence: verbal and mathematical analogies, spatial and mathematical patterns, classification, visual and logical reasoning, series, critical thinking, problem-solving and family relationships.
  • Language: comprehension, vocabulary, parts of speech, tenses, sentence formation, spelling, synonyms, antonyms, idioms, singular/plural and related language skills based on topics dealt with up to Class V.
  • General Knowledge: India and its symbols, art and culture, awards and personalities, defence, sports, organisations, solar system, environment, water cycle, energy, farming, food, health and everyday science.

Class IX: focus areas

  • Mathematics: rational numbers, linear equations, geometry, triangles, Pythagoras theorem, squares and cubes, percentages, profit and loss, algebra, interest, mensuration, data handling, statistics and probability.
  • Intelligence: analogies, patterns, classification, visual and logical reasoning, series, critical thinking and family relationships.
  • English: comprehension, grammar, vocabulary, sentence correction, tenses, modals, narration, voice, clauses and sentence formation.
  • General Science and Social Science: follow the exact topic lists in Appendix III, including the prescribed science concepts, environment, agriculture, geography, resources, history and civics-related themes.

For a fuller class-wise topic overview, read the TASVIA AISSEE Class VI and IX syllabus guide. Always cross-check it against the current bulletin.

6. A practical preparation roadmap

The following is a suggested TASVIA study framework for the time between registration and the examination. It is a learning plan, not an official NTA timetable. Adjust the daily workload to the child's school schedule, current level and target class.

Phase 1 · Diagnose and organise5–18 November

Attempt a short baseline test. List strong and weak topics, set a realistic weekly timetable and complete the first round of core concepts.

Phase 2 · Build topic confidence19 November–16 December

Work through the official topic list in small units. Practise mathematics regularly and rotate language, reasoning and GK or the relevant Class IX subjects.

Phase 3 · Timed section practice17 December–6 January

Attempt mixed question sets under time limits. Review every error and practise transferring answers carefully to an OMR-style sheet.

Phase 4 · Full mock and correction7–20 January

Complete full-length practice papers at the applicable duration. Review accuracy, time allocation and recurring mistakes rather than chasing a score alone.

Phase 5 · Final revision21–30 January

Revise formulae, key facts and error-notebook topics. Keep practice lighter, confirm the admit-card instructions and protect sleep and routine.

Use the marks distribution wisely

For Class VI, Mathematics carries 150 of 300 marks. For Class IX, Mathematics carries 200 of 400 marks. Give mathematics steady attention, but do not neglect the other sections: a balanced score and meeting the applicable qualifying requirements both matter. The bulletin's qualifying rules differ by school route and category, so check the exact rule that applies to the school and admission channel selected.

Build an OMR habit gradually

  • Practise one question number at a time and match it with the correct OMR row.
  • Darken the chosen response as instructed; avoid stray marks and rushed corrections.
  • Keep a few minutes for checking that question numbers and marked responses align.
  • Use the current NTA instructions for permitted stationery and examination-hall rules.

7. Parent checklist: avoid common application mistakes

  • Do not estimate age from the child's current age; compare the exact date of birth with the official range.
  • Check whether the chosen school has seats for the candidate's class, gender and category before selecting it.
  • Do not confuse an existing Sainik School with an approved New Sainik School or assume their admission routes are identical.
  • Check the OBC-NCL Central List rule where relevant; do not select a category based only on a state list.
  • Check image formats, file sizes and certificate scans before uploading.
  • Choose the Class VI paper medium carefully and select four preferred city codes in the order requested.
  • Complete the fee-payment step and download the confirmation page. A bank debit without a generated confirmation page is not proof that the application is complete.
  • Keep the application number, password and parent contact details safe. Never share OTPs or passwords with an unknown person.
  • Use only the official NTA portal. The bulletin warns families against touts or people promising guaranteed admission or special treatment.
Important distinction: Appearing for or qualifying in AISSEE does not by itself guarantee admission. The applicable merit list, counselling, school-wise vacancies, medical fitness and original-document verification also matter. Follow the NTA and Sainik Schools Society instructions.

Frequently asked questions

When is AISSEE 2027?

The NTA bulletin schedules AISSEE 2027 for Sunday, 31 January 2027. Class VI is 2:00 PM–4:30 PM and Class IX is 2:00 PM–5:00 PM.

What is the last date for the regular application?

2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM, including successful fee payment. A late application window is listed from 3 to 6 December with an additional ₹500 late fee.

Can girls apply for Class VI and Class IX?

Yes, the bulletin permits girls to apply for both classes. Check the school-wise vacancy list; Class IX admission for girls is subject to available vacancies.

Is there negative marking?

No. The AISSEE 2027 Information Bulletin states that there is no negative marking for a wrong response.

Can a Class VI student write the paper in Telugu?

Yes. Telugu is one of the 13 language options listed for Class VI. The selected medium should be confirmed before final submission because the bulletin says the option cannot be changed after it is exercised.

Where should parents apply and check updates?

Use the official NTA AISSEE portal. The current Information Bulletin contains the full rules and appendices.
