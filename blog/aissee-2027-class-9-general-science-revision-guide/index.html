1. Understand the AISSEE 2027 Class IX paper

The Class IX examination is an objective, multiple-choice, paper-and-pencil test using an OMR answer sheet. The NTA bulletin lists 150 questions for 400 marks and a total duration of 180 minutes (3 hours). General Science is one of five sections.

No negative markingOfficial evaluation rule

The bulletin states that a wrong response does not attract negative marks.

The Class IX question paper is in English only. NTA does not publish a separate official time limit for the Science section, so any section-wise practice timing should be treated as a coaching strategy, not an exam rule.

Do not assume equal topic weightage. Appendix III lists the General Science topics and the section has 25 questions for 50 marks. The bulletin does not promise one question from every topic or publish a fixed topic-wise question distribution.

3. How to study each Science topic

Use the same four-step routine for every topic. It is more useful than reading a chapter repeatedly without checking what the student can recall.

Understand one concept.

Study a short textbook section and identify the main idea, key terms and one real-life example.

Organise the information.

Use a labelled diagram, process flow, comparison table or cause-and-effect map where it helps.

Recall without the book.

Explain the concept aloud in simple words or write five points from memory before checking the text.

Apply it through MCQs.

Answer a short question set, explain why the correct option fits and record the reason for each error.

Science questions often test understanding of a process or an everyday example. Learn the difference between metals and non-metals through properties and uses; understand how friction changes with surfaces; and connect pollution, conservation and climate topics to familiar situations.

4. An eight-week AISSEE Class IX Science revision plan

This is a suggested TASVIA plan, not an official NTA schedule or a claim that the listed groups carry higher marks. Adjust the pace to the student's school syllabus and current strengths.

Week 1 · 1–7 DecemberFuels and combustion

Coal and petroleum, combustion and flame, calorific value. Compare fuels and note the conditions needed for combustion.

Week 2 · 8–14 DecemberMaterials and electricity

Metals and non-metals, synthetic fibres and plastics, chemical effects of electric current, electroplating and artificial jewellery.

Week 3 · 15–21 DecemberForce, sound and light

Force, friction, pressure, types of friction, sound basics, reflection and dispersion of light.

Week 4 · 22–28 DecemberSpace and environment

Stars and solar systems, pollution of air and water, global warming and natural phenomena.

Week 5 · 29 December–4 JanuaryLife science foundations

Cell structure and function, reproduction in plants and animals, and microorganisms.

Week 6 · 5–11 JanuaryAgriculture and conservation

Cropping seasons, agricultural practices, conservation of plants and animals, biosphere reserves, national parks and sanctuaries.

Week 7 · 12–18 JanuaryAdolescence and hormones

Reaching adolescence, changes during puberty, endocrine glands and hormones. Use age-appropriate school-level notes and diagrams.

Week 8 · 19–24 JanuaryMixed revision and mock practice

Revisit all 25 topics, attempt mixed Science sets, revise diagrams and compare mistakes in the error notebook.

Final week, 25–30 January: keep revision light and targeted. Revisit the error notebook, practise a few familiar mixed questions, check the admit card and centre route, and protect sleep. Avoid starting large new chapters at the last minute.

5. Original General Science concept-check questions

Try these questions before reading the explanations. They are original TASVIA practice items based on the broad concepts in Appendix III, not questions supplied or predicted by NTA.

Q1. Which of the following is a fossil fuel?

A. Wind   B. Coal   C. Sunlight   D. Flowing water

Answer: B. Coal. Coal and petroleum are fossil fuels formed over long periods from ancient organic matter.

Q2. Which gas is essential for ordinary combustion?

A. Oxygen   B. Helium   C. Nitrogen   D. Neon

Answer: A. Oxygen. Fuel, oxygen and sufficient heat are the familiar conditions needed to sustain combustion.

Q3. Which cell structure controls the movement of substances into and out of a cell?

A. Cell membrane   B. Cell wall only   C. Vacuole only   D. Nucleus only

Answer: A. Cell membrane. The cell membrane regulates the movement of materials between the cell and its surroundings.

Q4. In general, friction acts in which direction?

A. Always upward   B. Opposite to relative motion or its tendency   C. Only downward   D. Only in water

Answer: B. Friction opposes relative motion, or the tendency of surfaces to move relative to one another.

Q5. Sound cannot travel through which of these?

A. Air   B. Water   C. Steel   D. Vacuum

Answer: D. Vacuum. Sound is a mechanical wave and requires a material medium to travel.

Q6. What is the basic purpose of electroplating?

A. To separate sand from water   B. To deposit a thin metal layer using electric current   C. To freeze a metal   D. To produce sound

Answer: B. Electroplating uses an electric current to deposit a thin layer of one metal on another object.

Q7. Kharif crops are generally associated with which season?

A. Monsoon   B. Mid-winter only   C. Late winter only   D. No particular season

Answer: A. Monsoon. Kharif crops are generally sown with the arrival of the monsoon and harvested after the rainy season.

6. Class IX eligibility and AISSEE 2027 dates

For admission to the 2027–28 academic year in existing Sainik Schools, a candidate should be 13 to 15 years old as on 31 March 2027, with date of birth from 1 April 2012 to 31 March 2014, both dates inclusive. The candidate must have passed Class VIII from a recognised school at the time of admission. Admission for girls is subject to availability of vacancies.

For approved New Sainik Schools, Class IX admission is limited to approved schools that have progressed to Class IX. The bulletin lists 40 such schools for 2027–28; school-specific vacancy and admission conditions must be checked in the official bulletin and on the concerned school's website.

7. NTA examination-centre instructions

  • Mode: paper-and-pencil examination using an OMR answer sheet. Follow the invigilator's directions and the printed answer-sheet instructions.
  • Timing: Class IX examination is 2:00 PM–5:00 PM on 31 January 2027. The last permitted entry is 1:30 PM; the reporting time is the time printed on the admit card.
  • Permitted documents: printed admit card with the required photograph and thumb impression, original valid photo ID and one passport-size photograph for the attendance sheet. The bulletin also permits a transparent water bottle.
  • Stationery: NTA says pens will be provided. Do not bring personal stationery such as pens, scales, writing pads, geometry boxes or erasers.
  • Other barred items: mobile phones, Bluetooth devices, earphones, calculators, watches, wallets, handbags, caps, goggles, ornaments and food items are listed as prohibited, with the stated exception for candidates with diabetes.
  • No storage facility: centres will not make arrangements to keep candidates' personal belongings. Leave barred items at home.
  • Scoring: there is no negative marking for a wrong response.

Use the admit card for the exact centre, reporting time and any candidate-specific instruction. NTA advises candidates and parents to keep checking the official portal and registered email/SMS for updates.

8. Parent checklist before the examination

Keep these checks together:
  • Confirm the date of birth against the official Class IX eligibility range.
  • Keep proof that the candidate has passed Class VIII from a recognised school by the time of admission.
  • Check the selected school's Class IX vacancy, gender policy and any school-specific admission conditions.
  • Review the admit card, centre address, reporting time and route before exam day.
  • Do not pack stationery or electronic items that the NTA bulletin lists as barred.
  • Use only the official NTA portal for application, admit card and examination updates. NTA states that it has not authorised agents or touts for AISSEE-2027.

For existing Sainik Schools, the bulletin sets minimum qualifying marks by category; approved New Sainik Schools have a separate qualifying rule. A qualifying score does not guarantee a seat because merit, vacancies, counselling, medical fitness and document verification also apply.

Frequently asked questions

1. How many General Science questions are in AISSEE 2027 Class IX?

The NTA AISSEE-2027 bulletin lists 25 General Science questions for 50 marks in the Class IX paper.

2. What is the AISSEE 2027 Class IX exam date and timing?

The examination is scheduled for 31 January 2027 from 2:00 PM to 5:00 PM. The reporting time and allotted centre must be checked on the candidate's admit card.

3. Is the Class IX question paper available in Telugu?

No. The AISSEE-2027 bulletin specifies English as the medium for the Class IX examination.

4. What is the age eligibility for AISSEE 2027 Class IX?

A candidate should be 13 to 15 years old as on 31 March 2027, with date of birth from 1 April 2012 to 31 March 2014, both dates inclusive, and must have passed Class VIII from a recognised school at the time of admission.

5. Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?

No. The AISSEE-2027 bulletin states that there is no negative marking for a wrong response.

6. Can candidates bring their own pens or stationery?

No. The bulletin lists stationery such as pens, scales, geometry boxes and erasers among barred items. NTA states that pens will be provided. Candidates should carry only the permitted documents and items listed in the bulletin and admit card.
